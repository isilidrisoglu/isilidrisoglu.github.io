**Welcome!** I am a PhD candidate in Political Science at the University of Pittsburgh. My research sits at the intersection of political violence and political methodology. I study civil wars, focusing on the strategic behavior of armed groups as they interact with the states they fight, their rivals, and civilians. My work examines terrorism and coercive violence, insurgent propaganda, and recruitment and competition among armed organizations. To study these questions, I combine formal models, computational text analysis, and quantitative methods. I am also interested in developing measurement and data collection pipelines using large language models.

My dissertation, *Three Essays on Insurgent Strategy*, combines formal models and computational text analysis to study how armed groups use violence to signal strength, propaganda to shape how audiences see them, and ideological positioning to compete for recruits. My research has been supported by an APSA Centennial Research Grant and has received the Best Graduate Student Paper Award at the Formal Models of Conflict Conference. My published work appears in *Conflict Management and Peace Science*.

I expect to complete my Ph.D. in Spring 2027. I hold an M.A. in International Relations and Political Methodology from the University of Pittsburgh. Previously, I earned an M.A. in International Relations and a B.A. in Political Science and Public Administration from Bilkent University.
